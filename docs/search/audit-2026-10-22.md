# Search data audit, 2026-10-22

Read-only census of live nostr data on `wss://search.brainstorm.world` (the search relay) and `wss://dcosl.brainstorm.world` (dcosl), taken 2026-10-22 between 21:12 and 21:38 UTC. Every number below comes from a full fetch or an exact NIP-45 COUNT; the filter behind each one is in [Method](#method).

## Summary

Places are the cleaner dataset and events are the denser one. The Food and Drink Places list has exactly 7,954 items, identical on both relays (same `d` values, same event ids). Every item has coordinates, four `g` geohashes (precisions 9, 6, 5 and 4), a category and a country. Locality is filled on 79.4% and cuisine on 52.1%. The list is spread thin: 3,017 distinct localities in 115 countries, only 3 localities with 50 or more places and 82 with 10 or more. The six demo cities hold 8 to 56 places each by locality string (New York 8, Nashville 10, Chicago 28, Austin 39, Funchal 54, San Francisco 56). So a "places first" search works for structured filters (near me, category, cuisine) everywhere but gives short result lists in most cities. Events give longer lists in large cities. 11,014 events start between 2026-10-22 21:32 UTC and 2026-11-21 (7,474 Event Index items and 3,540 native NIP-52); New York has 336 and San Francisco 233. Only 57.2% of those carry a `g` tag, so naming a city needs a fallback to the location text, and the counts depend on which feeds are imported: Le Havre (327, via Mobilizon) and Stuttgart (466, nearly all from one German church-education publisher) both rank in the top three. The concept hierarchy that "events second" or faceted search might lean on hardly exists. Across all 421 headers and 357,030 items there are zero `s`, `n` or `o` tags, and zero DList items with a `b` tag. `b` appears only on 69 headers, mostly pointers from copies to originals. What hierarchy there is lives in 2,916 older-style relationship items: 64 `IS_A_SUPERSET_OF` edges under 18 parents, at most 2 levels deep, all in firmware or test concepts (nostr relays, nostr kinds, relationship types, dogs). None of the 7 Dictionary concepts has a superset edge. Facets for places and events therefore have to come from item fields (`category`, `cuisine`, `t`, `g`, `start`), not from a concept tree. Books, claimed at about 200,000, number 159,054.

### How a city was assigned

Each event gets the first of these that works:

1. **`g`**: decode the longest valid `g` and take the nearest of 270 hand-entered city anchors within 40 km.
2. **Online**: the location text says online, Zoom, virtual or webinar.
3. **Text**: the `location` text contains an anchor's name or alias as a whole word, matched without regard to case or accents (for example "Brooklyn" maps to New York, "München" to Munich).
4. **Zone tag** (Event Index only): the item's city-zone `t` tag, such as `new-york`, which names the place the feed was seeded from.
5. Otherwise a bucket: "has g, no anchor within 40 km", "location text not matched" or "no location".

How far to trust it:

- Where both `g` and text gave a city, they agreed on 3,143 of 3,243 Event Index items (96.9%) and 673 of 692 native events (97.3%).
- The zone tag agreed with `g` on 2,652 of 3,089 Event Index items (85.9%). The 1,644 items named by zone alone are therefore wrong about 14% of the time, mostly Luma city feeds that list events in nearby towns.
- 40 km folds suburbs into the city: San Francisco includes Oakland and Berkeley, Seattle includes Bellevue.
- The anchor list was extended once, after the first pass showed two large unnamed clusters (Le Havre/Rouen and towns in Baden-Württemberg). Smaller towns stay in the buckets.
- Unnamed: Event Index 751 of 7,474 (10.0%: 325 with `g` but no anchor, 224 with unmatched text, 177 with no location, 25 online). Native 1,354 of 3,540 (38.2%: 334, 721, 94 and 205).

Assignment by method: Event Index: `g` 4,018, zone 1,644, text 1,061, online 25, no city 726. Native: `g` 1,600, text 586, online 205, no city 1,149.

## Method

Relays: `wss://search.brainstorm.world` (vespa-relay) and `wss://dcosl.brainstorm.world` (strfry). Requests were read-only: REQ, CLOSE and COUNT only, with no AUTH, EVENT or signing. The scripts were one-off and are not committed, and the raw dumps were not kept: the steps and filters below are enough to repeat the census. 989 requests in total, of which 203 REQ and 413 COUNT went to the search relay and 370 REQ and 3 COUNT to dcosl. There were 5 timeouts: dcosl does not answer NIP-45 COUNT (3), and the search relay drops filters with 5,000 tag values (2 probes). Requests were sequential, with 250 to 800 ms between pages.

Relay behaviour observed:

- The search relay refuses filters without a NIP-50 token (`auth-required`), so every filter there carries `"search":"include:spam"`.
- The search relay returns at most 5,000 events per REQ, newest first, and honours `since`/`until`. It answers COUNT, and COUNT matched full fetches every time (39998: 421 = 421; 9998: 129 = 129; 31922 + 31923: 50,767 = 50,767; food: 7,954 = 7,954; 39999: 356,794 at the start = 356,794 fetched).
- dcosl returns at most 10,000 events per REQ and does not answer COUNT.

Full-fetch algorithm: page back with `until` = oldest `created_at` − 1. After every full page, refetch the boundary second with `since` = `until` = T, so that no event sharing T is skipped. A boundary second that itself fills a page is logged as an overflow. One overflow occurred: search, kind 39999, T = 1791242721, which holds 7,805 events (7,804 food items and 1 other). The overflow was filled with 20 REQs of `{"kinds":[39999],"#z":[FOOD],"#d":[≤400 d values from the dcosl dump],"since":T,"until":T}` and got 7,804 of 7,804. A top-up then fetched events newer than the fetch start.

## Caveats

- **Snapshot.** The relays change while you read them. Search's kind 39999 COUNT rose from 356,794 to 356,798 during the 4-minute fetch. The fetched set (356,799) is one larger than the final COUNT because one address was replaced mid-fetch; counts by address are unaffected. dcosl was fetched about 5 minutes before the search relay.
- **dcosl lags on Events.** dcosl is short 1,362 Events items and 11 Event Index items compared with the search relay, consistent with the Mobilizon resend still pending from the Event Index daily reconcile. Search is short 232 Books.
- **City names for events are heuristic.** The anchors are hand-entered (270 cities). Towns outside them land in buckets: 10.0% of Event Index and 38.2% of native upcoming events are unnamed. Text matching can misfire, for example on a street named after a city; where `g` and text overlap they agree about 97% of the time. Zone-tag assignment is about 86% accurate. City totals also reflect which feeds are imported (Le Havre, Clermont-Ferrand and Clonmel come from Mobilizon and Gancio instances; Stuttgart from one publisher), not where events are most common.
- **Native duplicates are not removed.** At least 14% of native upcoming events repeat a (title, start) pair, mostly aggregator mirrors. Event Index items that name an aggregator copy (`["a",…,"copy"]`) are also counted.
- **"Start in window" uses `start` only.** Multi-day events that began before now are not counted. The 102 date-only Event Index items and kind 31922 events are compared by UTC date.
- **Food localities are BTC Map's `addr:city`.** They are inconsistent across countries ("Washington" is DC; "Berlín" is the town in El Salvador; New York places mostly lack a locality). The radius columns are the better density measure.
- **The cuisine tag holds the first value only.** Use `t` to see every cuisine value. 7 items lose a cuisine value to `t` deduplication, where the cuisine equals the category text.
- **Kind 9999 counts are event counts.** Non-addressable items can repeat. Song From PodcastIndex has 1,144,212 events but only 915,383 distinct `t` values.
- **Labels: only `#a` and `#e` were checked.** A label that names a DList item only in content, or through a `#p` or `#r` tag, would not be counted. Kind 9999 item ids (1.45M) were not checked against labels.
- **The superset census reads only `relationshipType` items.** The old relationship-item form appears with several conventions (multiple copies by different authors, mixed-case type names); every distinct address is counted once, so copies are counted separately.
