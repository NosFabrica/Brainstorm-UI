## Why each miss happened

**The tool, 8 entries.** What a result doesn't carry decides most misses:

- a calendar event's start, location and title;
- an RSVP's event and status;
- a place's opening hours, and its category, cuisine and town, so right answers stay unconfirmed;
- a review's stars and place.

`since:` and `until:` can't stand for a start date. `search_link` drops the `kinds` argument. `found` at the limit gives
no sign of more. All of this goes to A6.13.f (memo there).

**Missing data, 3 entries.** Place reviews are the act behind "my network" and behind "cozy", and there are nine on the
relay.

**The guide, no entry alone.** But the Claudes had to discover things the guide didn't say:

- `since:` and `until:` are about when an event was written;
- to keep to one list, use `from:` its items' signer;
- words match field values;
- quoting stops loose matches;
- "my network" needs an act, and events have none that can be joined.

Each is now in `ui/search/find-me`, in the same pull request as this record.

## How it was run, and what to trust

- **Answering.** Each Claude was told the day, the reader and the windows:
  - today: 31 October;
  - this weekend: 31 October and 1 November;
  - this week: 26 October to 1 November;
  - next 7 days: from now to 7 November;
  - upcoming: from now.

  It was told to read `ui/search/find-me` and `ui/search/syntax` first, and to use only `brainstorm_search`,
  `tapestry_lists` and the guides. Each answer lists its searches, up to ten results with links, what it couldn't check,
  and the answer it would give.

- **Scoring.** Each result was read from the relay by its address, under `include:spam`:
  - a place is an item of Food and Drink Places, and an event is a calendar event or an Event Index item;
  - the town is checked against a place's `locality` or an event's `location`, the property against the item's own
    tags, and the time against `start` and `end`;
  - an expected event counts only if it falls in the window the question was given.
- **One caveat.** The scorer's test file sat in the folder the Claudes worked in, and it named the expected New York
  bar. Two of the three reported not opening it. The places Claude listed the searches that found that bar: `from:` the
  list's signer, `"new york" bar`, and the reader's own review of it.
